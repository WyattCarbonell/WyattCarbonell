My name is Wyatt Carbonell, and I have become an amateur software developer while working on research projects across mathematics and physics. I was awarded my BA in 2024 from Connecticut College, majoring in physics and mathematics with minors in astronomy and computer science. I then went on to read for my MSc in mathematical physics at the University of Edinburgh, awarded in 2025, and my MSc in mathematical sciences at the University of Oxford, awarded in 2026. The software I display on my GitHub reflects the diversity of my work, including data analysis and plotting software for astrophysics; simulations for cosmology (see github.com/MichaelSeifert/cosmic_anisotropy), particle physics, and biophysics research; and encryption software that I helped develop and went on to win first place at MIT's BeaverWorks competition (private by request, but at github.com/WyattCarbonell/bwsi_2rot13). Unfortunately, many of my early works have been lost to time, including some hardware projects (robotics and course projects), a video game I developed in a group for an undergraduate course titled 'A Cat Out of Time,' and some early experiments with machine learning. The bulk of my experience has been in Python, though I claim varying competency in Java, C, C#, C++, and Mathematica from assorted projects and courses over the years; I also have a reasonable amount of experience with terminal scripts and high-performance computing clusters.
